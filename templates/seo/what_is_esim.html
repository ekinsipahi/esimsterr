{% trans "What is an eSIM?" %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} An eSIM is a SIM card that is already inside your phone. Instead of collecting a piece of plastic from a shop, you scan a QR code and the mobile plan downloads onto a chip that was soldered in at the factory. Everything else about it behaves like a SIM, because it is one. {% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "How an eSIM actually works" %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} A physical SIM is a small computer with a number on it. It stores the credentials that tell a mobile network who you are and which plan you have paid for. An eSIM stores exactly the same credentials, but it is a permanent chip inside the handset that can be written to over the internet rather than a card you swap. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} The QR code you scan is not the plan itself. It is an address and an activation token: your phone reads it, connects to the provisioning server it points at, downloads the profile and installs it. That is why installing an eSIM needs an internet connection and why you should do it on Wi-Fi at home rather than after you land. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Once installed, the profile sits in your phone's settings next to your existing line. You can turn it on and off, choose which line carries mobile data, and delete it when the trip is over. Most modern phones can store several profiles and keep two of them active at the same time. {% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "Is an eSIM the same as dual SIM?" %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} They are related, and the two terms get muddled constantly. Dual SIM describes a phone that can run two lines at once. An eSIM is one way of supplying one of those lines. Most recent phones are dual SIM precisely because they pair one physical tray with an eSIM, and some newer models support two eSIM profiles active together with no tray at all. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} For a traveller this is the whole point. Your normal SIM stays where it is and keeps your number reachable for calls, SMS and the bank codes that follow you everywhere. The travel eSIM carries the data. Nothing about your identity, your WhatsApp account or your contacts changes. {% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "What an eSIM is not" %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} It is not a VPN and it does not hide anything. It is not a second phone number unless you specifically buy a plan that includes one; travel data plans are data-only, which is why calls go over WhatsApp, FaceTime or Telegram. And it is not tied to a country: the same chip in your phone can hold a plan from anywhere. {% endblocktrans %}
